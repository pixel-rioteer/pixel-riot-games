FPS Review

Call of Duty: Warzone

9.1/10

Warzone is a battle royale built around pressure. Every match asks you to make decisions quickly: where to drop, whether to push a nearby squad, when to rotate, when to spend cash, which loadout to trust, and how much risk to take before the gas forces everyone together.

The shooting is the heart of the game. Weapons feel sharp, recoil control matters, and a good loadout can completely change the way you approach fights. Long-range players can hold angles and play patiently, aggressive squads can hunt contracts and force engagements, and balanced teams can mix recon, movement, and utility to control the late game.

Warzone is strongest with friends. Communication turns a messy fight into a plan: one player breaks armor, another watches the flank, someone calls the rotation, and the squad decides whether to reset or finish the fight. Solo play can still be exciting, but the game really comes alive when a team is working together.

The downside is that Warzone can change fast. Weapon balance, map updates, movement tuning, and seasonal changes can make a favorite strategy feel weaker overnight. That can be frustrating, but it also keeps competitive players returning to learn the new meta.

Best Parts

Strong gunplay, smart squad strategy, intense endgames, loadout creativity, and a huge skill ceiling.

Watch For

Balance changes can shift the meta quickly, and new players may need time to learn rotations and positioning.

Verdict

Warzone is not just about aiming well. It rewards planning, teamwork, timing, confidence, and knowing when to leave a bad fight. For players who enjoy competitive FPS games with high-stakes moments, it remains one of the most exciting choices.
