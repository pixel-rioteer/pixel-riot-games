RPG Review

Elden Ring

9.7/10

Elden Ring is a massive action RPG about curiosity, danger, and discovery. It does not constantly tell you where to go or what to do. Instead, it puts a strange world in front of you and trusts you to follow whatever catches your eye: a ruined tower, a distant castle, a cave entrance, a wandering boss, or a glowing item sitting somewhere it probably should not be.

The world design is the reason the game feels special. The Lands Between is open, but it is not empty. Almost every direction hides something: weapons, spells, upgrade materials, NPC quests, dungeons, lore, bosses, or entire underground areas. That sense of discovery makes progress feel personal, because two players can take completely different routes and still feel like they are playing the game correctly.

Combat is demanding but flexible. You can build around heavy weapons, quick dexterity attacks, sorcery, incantations, shields, bleed, frost, summons, bows, or hybrid setups. Bosses can be brutal, but the game usually gives you options. If one fight feels impossible, you can explore, level up, upgrade gear, change your build, or come back with a new plan.

It is not a gentle game. Some questlines are obscure, some bosses are punishing, and new players may need patience. But that difficulty is also part of what makes victory feel earned. When Elden Ring clicks, every upgrade, shortcut, dodge, and final hit feels meaningful.

Best Parts

Exploration, boss design, build variety, secrets, atmosphere, and the feeling that every direction matters.

Watch For

The game can be punishing, quests can be hard to follow, and some players may want a clearer path.

Verdict

Elden Ring is one of the strongest open-world RPGs because it turns exploration into the main reward. It is challenging, mysterious, and sometimes unforgiving, but it gives players an unforgettable world and the freedom to overcome it in their own way.
